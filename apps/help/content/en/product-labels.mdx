Every product and variant can carry a barcode. Ebda Store makes one for you, or you can type or scan your own, then prints labels you can stick on the stock. Product labels belong to the **Barcode scanning** feature (Settings, QR codes & barcodes, Product labels).

## Barcodes

- **Made for you.** A product without variants gets a code, and so does each variant, when it is created or imported. For items you already have, press **Generate missing barcodes**. These are EAN-13 codes in the range stores use for their own stock (they start with 20), so they never clash with a code on a real retail product.
- **Your own codes.** Type or scan an EAN-13 (13 digits) or UPC-A (12 digits): the last digit is a check digit and a code with a wrong one is refused, so a typo cannot become a label nobody can scan. Any other text up to 48 letters, digits and symbols is accepted as a Code 128 barcode.
- **One code, one item.** A code can belong to only one product or variant in your store. If it is already used, you are told which item has it.

## Print labels

1. Open Settings, then QR codes & barcodes, then **Product labels**.
2. Tick the products and variants and set how many copies of each.
3. Choose what goes on the label: name, variant, price, the digits under the bars and, if you want both codes on one label, the **Product page QR**.
4. Choose the size: A4 sticker sheets (3 x 7, 4 x 10, 5 x 13) or a thermal printer (58 mm and 80 mm rolls, 40 x 30 mm and 50 x 25 mm labels).
5. Download the **PDF** or press **Print**.

## Barcode and QR on one label

Tick **Product page QR** and each label shows the barcode for your till and a QR code that opens that product in your own store (your custom domain when you have one, otherwise your-store.ebda.app). A phone scan of a store barcode can match unrelated items online, so the QR is the code a customer should scan.

Both codes keep a size that scans. This works on the A4 3 x 7 and 4 x 10 sheets, the 58 mm and 80 mm rolls, and the 40 x 30 mm and 50 x 25 mm labels. On the A4 5 x 13 sheet the label is too small for both: the page tells you, and you can choose a bigger label or untick the QR to print the barcode only. A very long custom domain makes the QR bigger, so it can also be refused on the smallest labels.

Barcode style: plain bars (a true EAN-13 when the code is one) or one of the 24 decorative shapes. A shape always keeps a real Code 128 scan strip, but it needs a tall label, so shapes work on the 58 mm and 80 mm rolls and small labels use plain bars.

<Callout title="Every label is checked">
  Before a file is made, each barcode is read back the way a scanner would. If the bars would be too
  thin to print or cannot be read, the page tells you and nothing is created. Still print one label
  and scan it with a phone before you print a whole sheet.
</Callout>
