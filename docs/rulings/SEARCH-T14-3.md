# SEARCH-T14-3

> Section: 2026-10-16 — Search program, PR 4, Task 13 (quality eval): SEARCH-T13-\*

Ruling SEARCH-T14-3 (coordinator decision, implemented in Task 14 round 2) — a product query made ONLY of words under 3 characters (`xl`, `tv`, `قط`, `12`) matches the START of a word, index-backed: `doc LIKE 'ab%' OR doc LIKE '% ab%'` inside `ebda_product_search_ids` (the `p_word_start` argument; the SKU arm of the admin search becomes a SKU prefix). Evidence: a 2-character word has no trigram to look up as a substring, so the match normalised every product of the store, ~70 ms at 2,000 products and 0.56-0.75 s at 20,000 (curl, storefront and typeahead alike); the same GIN trigram indexes serve the word-start form (`" ab"`/`"  a"` trigrams), measured: 2-character storefront search at 20,000 products as `ebda_app` under the index-scan-counter test, k6 short-word mix p95 7 ms cached / 243 ms uncached-with-embeds at 20,000 (was ≥ 560 ms per single query). Typeahead now accepts ONE character (route, service, web client and `SearchAutocomplete` floors 2 → 1); a 1-character query returns only word-start matches. The storefront LIST keeps its 1-character floor (empty page) unchanged. A query with any word of 3+ characters is unchanged (every word is still a substring, so a short word in a mixed query still matches anywhere). Review I-1 amendment: "starts a word" is a boundary list ORed over the same GIN index (`v_bounds` in the definer): the document start, after a space, after `/ ( - , .`, and after the Arabic one-letter prefix `ا` (so `رز` finds `أرز`, which folds to `ارز`) or the article `ال` (so `رز` finds `الرز`, `قط` finds `القط`); `xl` finds `S/XL` and `(XL)`. Residual, not covered: the other one-letter Arabic prefixes (`و ب ل ك ف`), other punctuation (`[ ] " ' + & _`), and any word that begins mid-token after a character the list does not name; a document-and-index rebuild that turns every non-alphanumeric into a space (option a) would cover them and was not done. Pre-launch behaviour change, accepted by the coordinator: a 2-character word no longer matches inside a word (`ab` no longer finds `tab`); a literal `%` or `_` typed alone matches only a word that starts with it. A one-letter word-start that starts a quarter of all words (`a`) is not selective, so the planner may scan: 25 results in < 450 ms at 20,000 products in the test store, evidence by result and time, not a counter. (Round 3 removed an owner-session exemption the definer briefly had: see SEARCH-T14-7.) Cost if wrong: a shopper who types a 2-letter fragment from the middle of a word gets nothing; the 3-letter fragment still works.
