Ruling M128-1 (audit M-128, supersedes the audit half of P09-1 d) - the admin refund writes its audit trail in compensating order: INTENT row, then money, then OUTCOME row. (a) `POST /payments/refund` writes `payment.refund_requested` with `auditLogStrict` (a new throwing sibling of the swallowing `auditLog`) on its own store transaction BEFORE `PaymentService.refund`; if that write fails the refund is refused with a 503 and an Arabic message saying no money moved, so no refund can leave without a durable trace. (b) A refund that throws closes the intent with a best-effort `payment.refund_failed` row. (c) After the money moved, the `payment.refunded` outcome row (one retry) and the payment event row are separate writes on separate transactions: neither can turn the answer into an error, and one failing never takes the other. Either failing raises the P1 `payment.refund.audit_failed` alert naming which row. (d) The money fact itself is the payment row plus its `refund_initiated` / `refund_completed` events written inside the service, so it is never at risk from the trail. Why: P09-1 d made the outcome write non-fatal, but `auditLog` swallows its own errors, so that failure raised no alert and left no intent behind. Evidence: tests/unit/payment-refund-audit-status.test.ts (5 tests fail on the old code: intent-before-money order, no intent row = no money, refund_failed closure, independent writes). Cost if wrong: while the audit table is down admins cannot refund (503, retry later), which is the intended fail-closed trade for money out.
