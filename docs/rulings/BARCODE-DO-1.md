# BARCODE-DO-1

Ruling BARCODE-DO-1 (2026-10-30, INFRA-CF-1 final integration) - in-store barcode allocation for a Durable Object store is two money ops, `barcodeFill` (`generateMissingBarcodes`, the labels page and the post-import fill) and `barcodeEnsure` (`ensureBarcode`, the auto code of a new product or variant), in `packages/repo/src/do/barcode.ts`. Each is ONE synchronous section: the next serial after the store's highest 20-prefixed EAN-13 over products and variants, the pick of barcode-less rows and the writes commit together, so the single-threaded actor replaces `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext(store||':barcode'))` and two requests are never handed one code. The pick and the remaining count follow the Postgres twin (live products without variants, every variant of a live product, oldest first, at most 500 a call, a deleted item never filled; the serial scan, like Postgres, does not filter deleted rows). `assertBarcodeAvailable` reads the store's own rows through `storeDb` and takes no lock; the partial unique indexes are the backstop. The post-import fill is no longer skipped for a DO store. Found on the way: workerd caps a GLOB pattern at 50 bytes, so the 57-byte "prefix 20 + 11 digits" pattern failed with `LIKE or GLOB pattern too complex` on workerd while it passed on node:sqlite; the scan is spelled with `length`, `substr` and a short `NOT GLOB`. Evidence: `tests/integration/repo-factory-barcode-contract.test.ts` (5 scenarios, Postgres vs the DO schema, equal traces: fill, limit and remaining, ensure, post-import fill, availability in both spellings), `apps/api/workers-do/barcode.test.ts` (real workerd: 30 concurrent fills of 40 products hand out 40 distinct codes; 25 concurrent ensures give 25 distinct codes and a repeat gives null; fills racing ensures stay distinct). Cost if wrong: a DO store could get a gap or a repeat in its in-store serial; the unique index refuses a repeat, so the cost is a failed generate that the merchant retries.
