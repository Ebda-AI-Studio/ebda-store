# NZ-MONEY-6

Ruling NZ-MONEY-6 (2026-11-11, Postgres removed from the Worker; plan `docs/plans/2026-11-11-nz-money-auth.md` M1-M3, M7) - the money paths of a store on its Durable Object move every atomic read-check-write into ONE DO money op and make the rest resumable: (1) a merchant-recorded InstaPay refund is the op `instapayRefundRecord` (order + latest confirmed InstaPay payment, dedupe on the normalised bank reference, remaining-amount check, payment update, `refund` payment event, order `payment_status` on a full refund, all in one `transactionSync`); the order status flip to `refunded` runs after it and a replay of a full refund on an order not yet `refunded` runs the flip again (forward completion), a committed record keeps its R2 screenshot while an uncommitted one or a replay frees its own; (2) the operator refund match is the existing DO `matchRefundToClaim`, now a money op `refundMatchClaim` scoped to the route's order (a claim of another order is 404); (3) gateway refunds needed no port: `PaymentService.refund` takes the DO branch (`refundOnDo`: claim, provider, outcome, `refundKey`) and all four callers pass the store id; (4) the WhatsApp COD outcome and the cancel after erasure read the order through `storeDb` (no row lock across two DO calls), run under `runInStoreTenantContext` with `noPgTx` for the deferred fanout, and treat a status transition the order state machine refuses as "not acted" (audited with the refusal code, never a retry loop). Found on the way and fixed at the source: `OrderService.applyRefundEffects` re-armed the stuck-reversal claims through Postgres after every DO refund (`order.updateMany` / `return.updateMany`), so every DO refund to `refunded` (manual status change and the InstaPay full refund) answered 500; the DO already arms both in the refund's own section, so only a Postgres store arms them there. Evidence: `apps/api/workers-test/merchant-orders-do.test.ts` third case (red on the old code: five 500s on the refund routes and POSTGRES_REMOVED from the WhatsApp consumer; green with zero Postgres reaches), `packages/repo/test/do-payments.test.ts`, `tests/unit/instapay-refund-do.test.ts`, `tests/unit/wa-store-events-consumer.test.ts`. Cost if wrong: a crash between the refund record and the order flip leaves a refunded payment on a non-refunded order until the merchant retries the same reference (which converges it); no money is ever recorded twice.
