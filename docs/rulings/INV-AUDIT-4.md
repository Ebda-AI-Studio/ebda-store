# INV-AUDIT-4

> Section: 2026-11-14 inventory audit P1-4 (branch `fix/inventory-p1-audit`)

Ruling INV-AUDIT-4 - the reorder projection's demand signal (`sumOrderQty` in `reorder-scan.service.ts`, the 7- and 28-day velocity) counts every order whose status is not `cancelled` or `refunded`, whatever its payment status, net of the quantities of COMPLETED returns (type `return`/`refund`) against those orders' lines, floored at 0. Demand is what customers asked to buy, not what has been settled: a COD order stays `unpaid` until delivery settles it (MONEYB-7), so the old `paymentStatus = 'paid'` filter understated a COD store's velocity and its suggestions. The change is scoped to the projection; revenue, analytics and the GL keep their own paid-based definitions (a separate decision). The audit's "refunded" case is handled by the status filter (a full refund flips the order to `refunded`) and by the returns netting (partial returns); `failed`-payment orders still count while they are open holds (their stock is released by the auto-cancel sweep, INV-AUDIT-2, after which they drop out). Evidence: `tests/unit/reorder-scan-demand.test.ts` runs the real DO query layer (prisma-lite over the StoreDO schema): COD counted, cancelled/refunded excluded, completed returns netted, pending returns not, window and per-variant scoping; red before the fix (3 vs 9, 12 vs 10, 0 vs 3). The Postgres raw-SQL branch mirrors it but was not run against a live Postgres. Cost if wrong: a store with many orders that are never cancelled but never fulfilled sees an inflated velocity and orders slightly more than it sells; the suggestion is only a draft the merchant approves.
