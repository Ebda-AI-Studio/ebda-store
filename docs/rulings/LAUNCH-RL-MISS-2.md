# LAUNCH-RL-MISS-2

> Section: Launch blocker 2026-11-09 (branch `fix/public-limiter-counts-success`): class sweep of the failures-only limiters

Ruling LAUNCH-RL-MISS-2 - the two other failures-only limiters, the IP-wide sign-in limiter (`rl:auth-ip-fail`, 60 failures per 15 minutes per IP, on login, change-password, reset-password and MFA verify) and passkey finish (`rl:passkey-finish`, 20 failures per 15 minutes per IP), KEEP express-rate-limit's atomic count-then-give-back; the give-back is fixed where it is made: `CfRateLimitStore.decrement` hands its `unhit` Durable Object call to `waitUntil` and never rejects. They share the root cause of LAUNCH-RL-MISS-1 (the give-back runs after the response and was not kept alive on the Worker), so honest sign-ins behind one carrier NAT would have used up the failure budget of everyone on that IP. They are not moved to read-then-count because here the count is the control: a burst of parallel wrong passwords with a different email each must not get past the IP-wide cap, which only the atomic count guarantees. Evidence: sweep of `skipSuccessfulRequests` / `requestWasSuccessful` / `.decrement(` across `apps`, `packages` and `scripts`: three limiters (store-miss, auth-ip-fail, passkey-finish), one store method; `tests/unit/public-limit-prefetch.test.ts` fails on the old code (`decrement` is not handed to `waitUntil`, a failed give-back rejects); `apps/api/workers-test/failure-only-limiters.test.ts` on workerd: 8 successful customer sign-ins from one IP leave `rl:auth-ip-fail` at 0, 3 failed ones leave it at 3. Passkey finish has no workerd case: a successful finish needs a WebAuthn assertion signed by a registered authenticator; it goes through the same `decrement`.

Accepted residual: a sign-in still holds one slot while it is in flight, so more than 60 sign-ins in flight at once from one IP (20 for passkey finish) would see a 429 until some finish. Not verified in production: the drop of a give-back does not reproduce on local workerd, and production cannot be probed without customer credentials (the `RateLimit-*` headers of a login response belong to the per-account limiter that runs after this one). What the production check of LAUNCH-RL-MISS-1 does show is that a Durable Object call handed to `waitUntil` after the response lands (the miss count falls on consecutive 404s).

Cost if wrong: if `waitUntil` does not keep the give-back alive in production, 60 successful sign-ins from one carrier IP within 15 minutes lock that IP out of sign-in until the window ends, exactly as before this change.
