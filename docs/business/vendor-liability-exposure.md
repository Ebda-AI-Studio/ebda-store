# Vendor Liability Exposure — Critical-Path Dependencies

**Finding D2 — Builder Reality Audit, 2026-07-09**
**Status:** Draft for Ahmad review. Not legal advice.

## 1. Purpose

Force Website Builder (FWB) is a Kuwait-based multi-tenant SaaS ecommerce platform. Every merchant's data — orders, customers, addresses, payment metadata, product catalogs — lives on infrastructure FWB does not own or operate. This document catalogs the **contractual liability cap** each critical-path vendor carries, so Ahmad/ForceAI understands the real financial exposure if a vendor loses data, leaks data, or goes down for an extended period, and what actually mitigates that exposure today (contract vs. our own DR posture).

> **Disclaimer — read before relying on any figure below.** All liability-cap language quoted here was pulled from each vendor's **current published, self-serve Terms of Service / DPA as of 2026-07-09** via live fetch of their legal pages. These are the _standard clickwrap terms a new self-serve signup would accept_ — they are **not confirmed to be the exact version FWB's account actually clicked through or is bound by**. Vendors revise ToS periodically (grandfather clauses, versioning, negotiated enterprise addenda) without always re-prompting existing customers. **Before this document is treated as authoritative for risk/insurance decisions, someone must pull FWB's actual account-level agreement (billing portal → legal/agreement history, or the enterprise contract if one was negotiated) for each vendor and diff it against what's quoted here.** This document is a starting point for that verification, not a substitute for it, and nothing here is legal advice — a real liability/indemnification assessment should involve counsel, especially for the PSP relationship (MyFatoorah) where Kuwait-specific consumer-protection and payment-services regulation may override generic ToS liability caps.

## 3. Per-vendor detail

### Neon

Neon's legal terms now flow through **Databricks' Master Cloud Services Agreement** ([databricks.com/legal/mcsa](https://www.databricks.com/legal/mcsa)) following the Databricks acquisition of Neon — this is worth knowing on its own, since it means Neon's contractual posture is now backed by a much larger enterprise vendor than "Neon the startup." Effective 2026-02-20:

- **§6.3 (general cap):** aggregate liability of each party limited to fees paid for the services giving rise to the liability **in the 12 months preceding the first incident**.
- **§6.4 ("super cap"):** liability for **unauthorized disclosure of Customer Content is 2× the 12-month fee cap** — a meaningfully better position than the flat 1× cap most of the other vendors carry, reflecting that this is the vendor holding the most sensitive data.
- **§6.6:** Beta/free-trial services are separately capped at a flat **$5,000**.
- DPA at [neon.com/dpa](https://neon.com/dpa) references sub-processors at [neon.com/subprocessors](https://neon.com/subprocessors), and the underlying Databricks DPA ([databricks.com/.../dpa-20230721.pdf](https://www.databricks.com/sites/default/files/legal/dpa-20230721.pdf)) for GDPR mechanics — Neon/Databricks claim GDPR, ISO 27001/27701, SOC 2, and HIPAA compliance certifications. Data residency/region options were not clearly specified in the fetched excerpt — **action item: confirm which Neon region FWB's production DB runs in and whether that satisfies any data-residency expectation under Kuwait Law 13/2023** (Kuwait law does not currently mandate in-country storage for most categories, but this should be confirmed against the client contracts, not assumed).
- Breach notification language in the fetched excerpt was generic ("notification will be in accordance with the terms of the Agreement") — no specific SLA timeframe (e.g. 72 hours) was found; **action item: pull the actual Databricks DPA breach-notification clause.**

### Railway

[railway.com/legal/terms](https://railway.com/legal/terms) — "Limitation of Liability" section: liability capped at **the greater of $100 or fees paid/payable in the 12 months preceding the claim**; explicitly excludes indirect/special/incidental/punitive/consequential damages, lost profits, business interruption, **loss of data**, loss of goodwill. Note the **explicit "loss of data" exclusion** — Railway is not the source of truth for tenant data (Redis is cache-only), so this is a lower-stakes exclusion for FWB than it would be for a vendor holding primary data.
A separate [Enterprise Customer Agreement](https://railway.com/legal/enterprise-agreement) exists with the same 12-months-fees structure but is a negotiated document — **action item: confirm whether FWB's Railway account is on Pro self-serve or a negotiated Enterprise agreement**, since the latter can carry a higher or custom cap.
DPA exists at [railway.com/legal/dpa](https://railway.com/legal/dpa) (per search results — full text not independently fetched in this pass); sub-processors include Stripe (billing), Cloudflare (CDN), Google Cloud (infrastructure) per Railway's Trust Center. Primary processing is US-based; standard cross-border transfer language expected (not independently verified this pass).

### Cloudflare

[cloudflare.com/terms/](https://www.cloudflare.com/terms/), Self-Serve Subscription Agreement, effective **2025-09-12**: aggregate liability capped at amounts paid **in the 12 months prior to the claim**. The important nuance for FWB: **§2.6 zeroes out liability entirely for any "Free Services"** — "WE WILL HAVE NO LIABILITY FOR ANY HARM OR DAMAGE ARISING OUT OF OR IN CONNECTION WITH ANY FREE SERVICES." If any of FWB's Workers/R2/DNS usage sits on a free-tier product (as opposed to paid Workers/R2 usage), that specific product line carries **zero contractual liability**, not just a low one. **Action item: enumerate which Cloudflare products FWB actually pays for vs. uses on free tier, since the free-tier carve-out is a materially different (worse) risk posture than the 12-month-fees cap.**
This is the Self-Serve agreement; Cloudflare also offers Enterprise terms with negotiated SLAs and typically higher/different caps — not confirmed whether applicable here (Force AI's Vercel Pro/Cloudflare relationship per user memory suggests self-serve paid tiers, not an Enterprise contract, but this should be confirmed against the actual CF account).

### Resend

[resend.com/legal/terms-of-service](https://resend.com/legal/terms-of-service), effective **2025-12-31**: liability capped at "the amount paid for the products and/or services," with no indirect/punitive/special/incidental/consequential damages. The clause as fetched did **not** include an explicit "preceding 12 months" qualifier the way Cloudflare/Railway/Neon do — worth clarifying whether "amount paid" means lifetime spend, current billing period, or is meant to be read as 12-months-trailing by cross-reference elsewhere in the ToS (not confirmed in this pass).
DPA at [resend.com/legal/dpa](https://resend.com/legal/dpa): sub-processors published at [resend.com/legal/subprocessors](https://resend.com/legal/subprocessors) with **14 days' advance notice** before adding/replacing a sub-processor (customer right to object). Primary processing is **US-based** ("Customer acknowledges that Company's primary processing operations take place in the United States, and that the transfer of Customer's Personal Data to the United States is necessary"). GDPR transfer mechanism: EU SCCs (Commission Decision 2021/914), UK Addendum, Swiss framework, plus EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework certification. Breach notification: "without undue delay" — no fixed hour count. The DPA itself does not carry a separate liability cap; it explicitly incorporates the ToS cap by reference.

### PostHog

[posthog.com/terms](https://posthog.com/terms), effective **2026-06-29**: cap is **the greater of $1,000 or total fees paid to PostHog in the trailing one-year period** as of the claim date. Excludes indirect/punitive/incidental/special/consequential damages; the cap does not apply to breaches of the core license/usage-restriction sections or unpaid fees owed.
DPA at [posthog.com/dpa](https://posthog.com/dpa): sub-processors at [posthog.com/subprocessors](https://posthog.com/subprocessors), varying by the **data center location the customer selects** — FWB is configured for **EU hosting (`eu.i.posthog.com`)** per FWB's PostHog setup, which should mean EU-based sub-processors and processing, though the fetched DPA excerpt described data-center selection generically rather than confirming EU-specific sub-processor scope — **action item: pull the EU-specific sub-processor list to confirm no US-only sub-processors are in that chain.** GDPR mechanisms: EU-US/UK/Swiss Data Privacy Framework participation plus SCCs. Breach notification: "without undue delay." No liability cap or exclusion language appears in the DPA itself — it defers to the ToS cap above. Given FWB's PostHog config sends **no PII beyond `distinct_id`, IP capture disabled, session replay off**, the actual exposure here is low regardless of the contractual cap — data minimization is doing more work than the contract.

### MyFatoorah

**This is the vendor with the weakest verification in this audit.** Direct fetch of MyFatoorah's Kuwait ([kw.myfatoorah.com](https://kw.myfatoorah.com/En/KWT/Home/TermsAndConditions)) and general portal ([portal.myfatoorah.com](https://portal.myfatoorah.com/En/All/Home/TermsAndConditions)) terms pages both returned **HTTP 403** to automated fetch. A web search surfaced fragments (not a full clause) indicating:

- Force-majeure disclaimer (fire, natural disaster, banking-system failure, power/telecom outage, war, strikes, terrorist attacks, vendor/bank performance failures) — standard, no numeric cap tied to it.
- **Chargeback/fraud liability is contractually the merchant's**, not MyFatoorah's — "the Merchant maintains full liability and pledges to pay back MyFatoorah any funds deducted due to fraud or chargeback incidents," with MyFatoorah authorized to auto-deduct from merchant balances before settlement. This flows through to ForceAI's client (the store owner), not to ForceAI/FWB directly, but is relevant to how disputes get resolved operationally.
- No liability for third-party wallet providers (Apple Pay/Google Pay) used through MyFatoorah's flow.
- MyFatoorah can deactivate a merchant account "for any reason, without notice at any time" — an operational continuity risk independent of liability caps (a sudden deactivation would break checkout platform-wide for every store on that gateway).
- **No numeric liability cap clause (e.g. "12 months fees paid") was found anywhere in the fetched or searched material.** It is very likely MyFatoorah's actual binding terms with ForceAI are a **separately signed merchant/reseller agreement PDF**, not these public consumer-facing web pages — that document has not been located as part of this audit.

**This is flagged as the top action item below** — MyFatoorah is the vendor whose failure mode (checkout outage) is the most immediately revenue-critical of all six, and it's the one with the least contractual visibility in this audit.

## 4. Concentration risk

**Neon is the single point of concentration.** It holds every category of sensitive data FWB has (orders, customers, addresses, payment metadata) across every tenant, and it's the only vendor in this table where "the vendor's contractual cap" and "the actual cost of a real incident" are meaningfully far apart — a 12-month-fees (or even 2× for a breach) payout from Neon/Databricks would not come close to covering the cost of a multi-tenant PII breach notification/remediation event under GDPR or Kuwait Law 13/2023, let alone reputational damage across every merchant on the platform.

**The real mitigation for Neon is not the contract — it's FWB's own DR posture:**

- Daily local `pg_dump` via `~/.claude/scripts/backup-all-neon.sh` (9 rolling copies per project, independent of Neon, pulls the real DIRECT connection string via `neonctl`).
- Neon's built-in Point-in-Time Recovery (24h–7d retention window, separately confirmed/drilled per `feedback-neon-pitr-and-tested-restore.md`).
- Both restore paths have been drilled, not just configured (per existing standing practice) — this is the actual control that bounds the blast radius of a Neon incident, not the §6.3/6.4 liability language.

**Cloudflare R2 (product images) is the inverse case — low stakes, cap almost irrelevant.** Product images are not customer PII and are, in the worst case, **re-uploadable from merchant-supplied source assets** — there is no scenario where an R2 image loss is an unrecoverable or high-cost event, so the fact that Cloudflare's Free Services carve-out zeroes liability for any free-tier R2/Workers usage (§2.6) is a low-consequence gap, not a real risk.

**The general pattern across all six vendors:** every cap in this table (whether 12-months-fees, $1,000, $100, or $5,000-flat-for-beta) is trivial relative to the cost of a real multi-tenant data-breach incident (notification, regulatory exposure, remediation, reputational/churn cost). This is normal — it's how every cloud vendor's standard ToS is written, not a red flag specific to FWB's vendor choices. The gap between "vendor cap" and "real incident cost" is what cyber-liability insurance exists to backstop (see Action Items), and what FWB's own operational DR practices (Neon PITR + local dumps, redeployable stateless services, re-uploadable assets) are designed to make less likely to matter in the first place.

   - [ ] **MyFatoorah merchant/reseller agreement** — highest priority. Public ToS pages are 403-blocked and contain no visible liability cap; the checkout-outage blast radius is the most immediately revenue-critical of all six vendors. Get the actual signed PDF from the MyFatoorah merchant portal or account manager.
   - [ ] **Neon/Databricks account terms** — confirm FWB's account is actually bound by the MCSA quoted here (post-acquisition terms may have required re-acceptance) and pull the specific data-residency region in use.
   - [ ] **Cloudflare account** — enumerate which products (Workers, R2, DNS) are on paid vs. free tier, since free tier carries zero liability under §2.6, not just a low cap.
   - [ ] Railway, Resend, PostHog — lower priority (Railway is stateless/cache-only, Resend isn't the data source-of-truth, PostHog carries minimized/low-sensitivity data by design), but confirm account tier (self-serve vs. any negotiated terms) while doing the above.
2. **Decide whether any vendor tier is worth upgrading for a higher cap.** Given §4's conclusion (all caps are trivial relative to real incident cost regardless of tier), this is likely **not** the highest-leverage spend — but it's worth a deliberate yes/no rather than defaulting silently, especially for Neon given it holds everything.
3. **Cyber-liability insurance** is the real backstop for the gap between "vendor cap" and "actual breach cost" identified in §4 — this should be evaluated in a **separate document** (not conflated with vendor contract terms here), covering: coverage limits appropriate to FWB's tenant count/data volume, whether it covers regulatory fines under Kuwait Law 13/2023 and GDPR, breach-notification cost coverage, and business-interruption coverage for a Neon/MyFatoorah outage specifically (the two vendors whose downtime is most immediately business-critical).
4. **Confirm breach-notification SLA timeframes** for Neon specifically (the fetched material only said "in accordance with the Agreement" with no fixed hour count) — this matters for FWB's own downstream notification obligations to merchants/end-customers under GDPR (72-hour norm) and Kuwait Law 13/2023.
5. **Re-run this audit whenever a vendor tier changes** (e.g. if Neon, Railway, or Cloudflare moves from self-serve to a negotiated Enterprise agreement) — the caps and carve-outs quoted here are tier-specific and will not automatically carry over.
